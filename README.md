## Featured Research & Analytics Projects

### [Genome-Wide Association Analysis of Triglyceride Levels](https://github.com/pskooner/genetic-epidemiology-gwas-triglyceride-analysis)

**PLINK | R | Unix/Linux | Genetic Epidemiology**

* Conducted a genome-wide association analysis of triglyceride levels among 1,200 participants.
* Performed genotype quality control using missingness, minor allele frequency, and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium criteria.
* Conducted SNP-level linear regression adjusting for age, sex, and BMI.
* Generated regional association visualizations to investigate the strongest genomic signal.

### Predicting Parkinson's Disease Using Voice Recordings

**R | Machine Learning | Predictive Modeling**

* Developed and compared logistic regression, random forest, support vector machine, and multilayer perceptron models.
* Applied feature reduction, principal component analysis, normalization, and 5-fold cross-validation.
* Penalized logistic regression achieved an **AUC of 0.86**.
* Conducted feature-importance analysis to identify influential voice characteristics.

### Air Pollution and Asthma Burden in the United States

**R | Categorical Data Analysis | Epidemiology**

* Analyzed county-level CDC and EPA data to evaluate associations between air quality and asthma-related emergency department visits.
* Applied chi-square testing, Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel analysis, likelihood-ratio testing, and multivariable logistic regression.
* Evaluated geographic confounding and model diagnostics.

### Predictors of Lymph Node Burden in Stage II/III Colon Cancer

**R | Regression Modeling | Cancer Research**

* Analyzed randomized clinical trial data using multiple linear regression.
* Applied Box-Cox transformation, partial F-tests, interaction assessment, model selection, diagnostics, and PRESS-based cross-validation.
* Evaluated clinical and tumor-related predictors of lymph node burden.

## Master's Thesis

### Predicting Colorectal Cancer Screening Adherence Among U.S. Adults Using Multi-Year BRFSS Data

My thesis focuses on developing and comparing **survey-weighted statistical and machine learning models** for predicting colorectal cancer screening adherence among U.S. adults aged 45–75.

Methods include:

* Survey-weighted logistic regression
* Ridge regression
* LASSO
* Elastic Net
* Random Forest
* Gradient Boosting
* XGBoost
* Temporal validation
* Model discrimination and calibration
* SHAP and other explainable machine learning approaches

The project integrates **biostatistics, machine learning, complex survey methodology, cancer prevention, and population health research**.
